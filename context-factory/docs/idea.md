# Pic It

Share one event’s photos with the people who were there. Every member can shoot, see the roll, then **pic it** — pick the shots they want to keep or post.

Typical moments: birthdays, Christmas, debuts, parties, school events.

## Problem

Event photos live on whoever happened to have their camera out. They scatter through group chats and stories. Guests miss pictures they were in. Saving the good ones later is slow and incomplete.

## What a moment is

A **moment** is a shared roll for a single occasion, not a public social feed. The roll **stays** after the event; it does not expire.

- The host **creates** a moment.
- They **share** it so family or friends can join.
- **Every member can shoot** into that roll. There is no view-only role.
- Every member can **see** those photos.
- Each person **pics** the photos they want. Picks **stay in Pic It and copy to the device camera roll**.

The name is the action: pick it, from the shared roll.

## Core flow

1. **Create a moment** — name the occasion (and, later, time or place if we need them).
2. **Share the moment** — invite the people who should be in it.
3. **Take photos** — any member shoots into the moment, not only into the phone’s camera roll.
4. **See the roll** — members see photos others took, not only their own. The roll remains after the event.
5. **Pic it** — choose which photos to keep. Each pick is stored in Pic It and saved to the device camera roll.
6. **Share out** — send picks to Instagram Stories without leaving the app.

## What this is not

- A general backup library (Google Photos / iCloud).
- A public social network or follower feed.
- A photographer storefront.

Pic It is scoped to **this event, these people, then pick**.
